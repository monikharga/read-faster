A research team led by Associate Professor Ye Weiming of Peking University HSBC Business School and Li Qian, a faculty associate at Arizona State University's Walter Cronkite School of Journalism and Mass Communication, examined how the public discusses AI-assisted learning. However, concerns about inaccurate content, weakened independent thinking, plagiarism and unclear responsibility have also grown. The researchers therefore examined AI-assisted learning through three dimensions—cognition, behavior and ethics—and three stages of knowledge construction—acquisition, comprehension and application.

Using the Biterm Topic Model (BTM) and qualitative analysis, the researchers identified major public concerns about how AI is changing learning and knowledge construction. During knowledge acquisition, users appreciated AI's speed and ability to organize information, but factual errors and flawed reasoning created "credibility anxiety." During knowledge comprehension, AI made difficult concepts easier to grasp, while overreliance risked weakening independent reasoning and critical thinking. However, it also raised concerns about plagiarism, intellectual property, fairness and responsibility.
