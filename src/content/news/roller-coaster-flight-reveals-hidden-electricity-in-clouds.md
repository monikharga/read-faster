Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Measuring the electric charge in widespread, dull gray clouds could help scientists better understand droplets within them, a new study suggests. Because droplet size affects how much sunlight a cloud reflects back to space, understanding these differences may ultimately help improve knowledge of how clouds influence climate. Researchers at the University of Reading found that layer clouds, known to meteorologists as stratus or stratocumulus, tended to contain larger droplets when more electric charge was present at their tops, with droplet sizes increasing by around 10% across the range of charges measured.

Scientists have long suspected that atmospheric electricity might influence layer clouds, but detailed observations are lacking. This study found that clouds with more charge at their tops tended to contain larger droplets and that the charge measurements provided additional information about droplet size beyond conventional cloud measurements. The study used the FAAM Airborne Laboratory research aircraft, a modified jet fitted with a system for measuring atmospheric electricity.
