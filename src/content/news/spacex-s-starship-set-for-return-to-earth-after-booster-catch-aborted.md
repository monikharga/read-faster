Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: SpaceX's Starship spacecraft was "healthy" as it orbited the Earth and on track to return Saturday to its Texas launch site, where Elon Musk's company was planning to "catch" the vehicle for the first time. It was the 15th test flight of the world's biggest and most powerful rocket system, originally meant to feature a groundbreaking twin capture of both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship. After the midair separation of the two stages, the Super Heavy booster was initially set for capture at Launch Pad 2, SpaceX spokesman Dan Huot said.

"Starship and its heat shield are healthy," SpaceX said around seven hours after the mission started. Clayton Swope, an aerospace expert at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, called the mission a "mixed bag so far" after the failure to catch the Super Heavy, something SpaceX has done three times previously.
