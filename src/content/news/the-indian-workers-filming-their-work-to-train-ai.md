Mohammed Ashim is paid to film his hands at work Mohammed Ashim has been cutting hair and trimming beards for four years in Bengaluru. The operations team taught me that my hands always have to stay cleanly inside the frame, the lighting has to be sharp, and I can't tilt my head too abruptly while working." He wears the camera for five or six hours a day. Ashim's footage is collected by a local company, which might have hundreds of people filming their work.

"Every robotics company, every frontier AI lab, has a specific kind of data they want: industrial tasks, agriculture, household chores, small business work like pottery, garment-making, embroidery, candle-making. "We record hands, not faces, and where multi-camera rigs do capture more of the body or environment, we blur faces out. "Now clients are asking for four to six cameras rigged around a person's head to cover the entire scene, not just the hands.
