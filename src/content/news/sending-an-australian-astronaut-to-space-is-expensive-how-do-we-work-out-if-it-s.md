Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Australia is currently in talks with the European Space Agency (ESA) over potentially flying Katherine Bennell-Pegg on a future space mission. If accepted, Bennell-Pegg—who is the director of space technology at the Australian Space Agency and completed ESA training in 2024—would become the first officially funded Australian astronaut. Many are pointing out that sending a homegrown astronaut to space could benefit industry and the nation as a whole, inspiring younger generations.

There would be powerful symbolism in sending an Australian woman to space. The discussion raises an interesting point about what constitutes an Australian astronaut. To date, five Australian-born people have flown to space, two as NASA astronauts and three privately.

The most recent was physicist Elaine Hyde, the first Australian woman in space. But her 2025 Blue Origin flight was privately funded, and she hasn't been given the label of the first "female Australian astronaut."
