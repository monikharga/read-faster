I'm out with several of my graduate students, searching for a little bird that at first glance may look like a mouse running along the ground, since this bird doesn't like to fly. Rail!" yells out one of my grad students, and we stop pulling the rope to carefully approach the place where she saw the bird land after it flew a short distance away from the commotion.

Eastern black rails are sparrow-sized marsh birds that live in coastal wetlands along the Gulf and Atlantic coasts and in small inland populations, mostly in Colorado and Kansas. These cute little rails spend most of their time on the ground hunting for invertebrates and seeds to eat and using the vegetation to protect themselves from predators and the hot Gulf Coast summer sun.
