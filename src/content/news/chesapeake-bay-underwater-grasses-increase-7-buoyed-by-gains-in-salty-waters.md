Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Submerged aquatic vegetation (SAV), one of the Chesapeake Bay's most important indicators of ecosystem health, increased by 7% in 2025, according to researchers at William & Mary's VIMS & Batten School. The annual Baywide aerial survey reported a total of 89,385 acres (140 square miles) of underwater grasses in the Chesapeake Bay and its tidal tributaries, an increase of 6,134 acres (9.6 square miles) from 2024. The total is just shy of the Chesapeake Bay Watershed Agreement's 2030 interim goal of 90,000 acres (141 square miles) and represents approximately 45% of the 196,600-acre (307-square-mile) long-term goal.

The Polyhaline Zone, which includes the Bay's saltiest waters near its mouth, increased from 25,266 acres (39 square miles) in 2024 to an estimated 28,142 acres (44 square miles) in 2025, setting a new record for the second consecutive year. The moderately salty Mesohaline Zone expanded nearly 15%, from 33,033 acres (52 square miles) in 2024 to an estimated 37,879 acres (59 square miles) in 2025.
