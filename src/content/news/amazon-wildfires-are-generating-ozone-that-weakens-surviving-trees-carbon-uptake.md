Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Ozone pollution caused by Amazon wildfires is significantly reducing the rainforest's ability to absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere, new research reveals. This harms human health and interferes with plants' ability to absorb carbon dioxide (CO2) from the atmosphere. This means wildfires have a double impact on our climate: directly releasing CO2 from biomass burning and reducing the ability of remaining forests to capture and store CO2.

The new study, published in the journal Communications Earth & Environment, calculates that fire-driven ozone damage cuts Amazon carbon uptake by an amount equivalent to around 24% of the carbon emitted by the fires themselves. "We found this hidden effect is equivalent to around a quarter of the carbon released by the fires themselves—a significant loss to the Amazon's carbon budget that hasn't previously been considered."
