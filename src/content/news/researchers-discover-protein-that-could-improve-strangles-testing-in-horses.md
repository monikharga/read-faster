In a study published in the Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association, researchers found that a protein they called P92 could be used to more accurately detect antibodies against Streptococcus equi subspecies equi (SEE), the bacterium that causes strangles. The discovery could improve existing blood-based diagnostic testing and serve as the foundation for new tools that make detecting exposure to the bacterium easier. "Having fewer false-positive and false-negative results will improve diagnostic accuracy when trying to assess exposure to SEE," said Dr.

Serological testing offers another way to assess exposure to SEE by looking for antibodies against the bacterium in serum, the liquid portion of blood that remains after clotting. It can also help veterinarians determine which horses may have been exposed during an outbreak or assess horses that may be at risk for immune-mediated complications following exposure or vaccination.
