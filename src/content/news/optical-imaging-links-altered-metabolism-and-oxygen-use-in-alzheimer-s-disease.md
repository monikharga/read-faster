Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Alzheimer's disease is best known for its effects on memory and cognition, but the disease also involves changes in how brain cells produce and use energy. At the same time, changes in blood flow and oxygen availability may place additional demands on brain tissue. Researchers in Ammasi Periasamy's group at the University of Virginia (UVA) combined two forms of two-photon lifetime microscopy to measure cellular metabolism and oxygen levels in the cerebral cortex of mice.

By collecting both types of information at the same time, the researchers could examine how metabolic activity relates to oxygen availability within the same living tissue. To track cellular metabolism, the team used two-photon fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy, or FLIM. The second technique, phosphorescence lifetime imaging microscopy, or PLIM, provided information about oxygen.

Its phosphorescence lifetime changes in response to oxygen concentration, allowing the investigators to estimate the partial pressure of oxygen, or pO₂, in the brain's vascular network.
