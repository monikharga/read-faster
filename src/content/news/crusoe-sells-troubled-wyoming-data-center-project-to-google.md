Crusoe, an eight-year-old, Denver-based data center developer recently valued at $30.9 billion, confirmed Friday it has sold an unfinished data center project in southeastern Wyoming to Google. Google did not disclose how much it paid Crusoe for the unfinished project, but it announced plans to invest $17 billion to develop the data center campus. Crusoe is developing another data center for Google near Amarillo, Texas.

The deal to sell the site to Google was first reported by Bloomberg in June, but the Wyoming setback didn't stop Crusoe from raising a $3.9 billion round in September. Crusoe makes money by leasing data center space to customers that bring their own GPUs, by renting out its own GPUs, and by selling compute power used to run AI models, known as inference. Meta is also a customer, and Bloomberg reported that the trading firm Jane Street signed a $13 billion, five-year cloud deal with Crusoe.
