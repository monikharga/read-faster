Ecologists have known for decades that urbanization generally lowers local diversity. By analyzing data from more than 10,000 bird species worldwide, we found that the latitudinal diversity gradient is three times weaker in cities than in natural areas—an enormous reduction in one of the world's most important ecological patterns. Cities had a third fewer bird species than natural areas overall, confirming that around the world, urbanization lowers bird diversity.

But tropical cities lost three times more bird diversity than high-latitude cities. One of the reasons tropical cities lost more species lies in the traits of tropical birds. Tropical communities have long been thought to contain more specialized species, and previous work has suggested that cities tend to favor generalists—jack-of-all-trades species like crows and Canada geese.
