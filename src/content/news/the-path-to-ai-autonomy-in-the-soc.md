In a recent a16z survey of CISOs at organizations with more than 1,000 employees, 36% said they are comfortable today letting AI agents act autonomously on low-risk security actions. Analyst time is the scarcest resource in the SOC, and every alert still means pulling evidence from several systems, reconstructing what happened and deciding whether it matters. Only 1% are comfortable with agents acting autonomously on most actions today, and only 7% expect to be a year from now.

Autonomy is a set of decisions made per stage of the workflow and per action, and the teams that get it right treat it as something an agent earns with evidence. Researchers worked this out decades ago, in a framework later applied widely in aviation and air traffic control, long before anyone was building SOC agents. Those are information acquisition, information analysis, decision and action selection, and action implementation.

Each function can sit at a different level of automation, from fully manual to fully automatic, within the same system.
