In a study published in the journal Communications Psychology, a team led by researchers at Penn State found that human users rated AI-generated empathetic responses higher in quality than human-generated empathetic responses, yet they preferred human empathy over AI. Nevertheless, people think that the actual content of what the large language models are saying is empathetic, interesting and validating, and so there's this paradox between what people want and what they choose to opt into, versus their actual evaluations of the material. Wenger: We called this effect the "AI empathy choice paradox," and it's this consistent pattern where people rate AI empathy or empathetic expressions from AI as higher in quality and more empathetic than those from real humans.

But despite this higher quality, people still prefer to receive empathy—even lower-quality empathy—from humans over AI. Wenger: For every response in our study, we asked people to rate how empathetic they found each response.
