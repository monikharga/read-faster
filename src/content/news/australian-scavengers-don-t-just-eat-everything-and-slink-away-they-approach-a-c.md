Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: When scientists talk about scavengers, you might imagine an animal sniffing out or stumbling across a carcass, eating as much as possible and slinking away. But like all foods, a dead animal holds a variety of macronutrients—carbohydrates, lipids (fats) and protein—all within the same carcass, or meal. So, which part of the carcass does a hungry dingo, wedge-tailed eagle or red fox eat first?

Like a bodybuilder aiming to hit a protein goal, wild spider monkeys and orangutans choose foods that allow them to maintain a stable intake of protein. If different parts of a carcass offer different nutritional rewards, then scavenging may be less like eating leftovers and more like choosing from a buffet. Three common and important scavengers in this area are dingoes, wedge-tailed eagles and red foxes.
