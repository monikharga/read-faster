The startup Tau Robotics is sending humanoid robots to clean San Francisco homes. Tau's long-term goal is to make autonomous cleaning robots a reality, but they're not quite there yet: For now, the robots are piloted remotely by humans, who watch what's going on via cameras mounted in the robots' heads and wrists. As the robots clean, they collect data that Tau will use to train up their more independent descendants.

At the time I booked, Tau was offering $30 for an hourlong cleaning with a single robot; now the charge is $60 per weekly cleaning with two robots, or $100 for a one-time visit. But the robots are physically capable of cleaning, and they're undeniably cute. Tau has armed each four-and-a-half-foot-tall robot with a 3D-printed "head" with antennae that allow it to communicate with Tau's headquarters, as well as two 3D-printed claw "hands" (the more humanlike alternatives apparently aren't durable enough, and claws do surprisingly well for most cleaning tasks).
