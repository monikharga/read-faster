Helen is a well-known figure in the AI safety and policy community, though she is perhaps best known for having served on the board of OpenAI, when she was one of four board members who voted to fire Sam Altman. That incident, now sometimes referred to as "the blip," ended with Sam coming back as CEO, Helen resigning, and a new board of directors. But it's worth remembering what Helen and the board were supposed to do: OpenAI's nonprofit board existed specifically to make sure the company didn't betray its mission to build safe AI by prioritizing profit and growth over safety.

The CEOs of these frontier labs have since called for a slowdown and asked Washington for help, but the White House's answer was a voluntary accord — one that relies in part on the kind of board accountability that failed at OpenAI. That includes how to think about China, which is the issue that looms large in every policy conversation about AI right now — especially now after the Jensen Huang affair.
