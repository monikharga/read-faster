An iMessage flaw could let a remote attacker join a group chat and receive its messages, Apple said yesterday, and it released updates to fix it. The updates also fix a separate iMessage flaw that could let someone added to a group access messages sent before they joined. The company's notes also do not say whether either flaw has been exploited, what an attacker would need, whether group members would see someone who joined this way, or whether every member must update to be protected.

The updates that fix the iMessage flaws also fix a flaw in Google's Brotli compression library. The flaw that could let a remote attacker join a group chat is tracked as CVE-2026-113961. The Brotli flaw, CVE-2026-111497, is an out-of-bounds write in the library's decoder that could let malicious input corrupt memory.

All nine updates fix Bluetooth and AirPlay flaws.
