Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Microscopic particles called nanoparticles hold promise for delivering cancer treatments directly to tumors, potentially making therapies more effective while sparing healthy cells. By attaching molecules that recognize this protein to nanoparticles, scientists have hoped to improve how the disease is detected and treated by guiding the particles directly to target cancer cells. Their study found that while the nanoparticles successfully targeted ovarian cancer cells in laboratory experiments, they were far less selective in animal models.

Instead, immune cells and other cells surrounding the tumors absorbed many of the particles, limiting their ability to reach the cancer cells. When complement proteins attach to nanoparticles, they can make the particles more likely to be absorbed by immune cells before reaching their intended targets.
