Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Plants begin responding to spaceflight almost immediately after leaving Earth, according to a new University of Florida study that captured the earliest stages of biological adaptation during a commercial suborbital mission. For this study, they designed an experiment to capture biological changes during different phases of a mission. "There is tremendous intrinsic value in the suborbital realm itself, including understanding rapid changes in biology during various phases of spaceflight, especially the first few minutes of being in space." For the mission, Ferl carried dozens of Arabidopsis plants in specially designed tubes attached to his flight suit that he activated at four different points during the trip to space and back: before launch, upon reaching microgravity, at the end of the weightless period as the vehicle began its descent and upon landing.

The coordinated operation allowed researchers to compare the plants exposed to spaceflight with identical plants that remained on Earth and to examine how the flight samples changed from one phase of the mission to the next.
