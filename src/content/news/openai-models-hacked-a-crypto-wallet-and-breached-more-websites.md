OpenAI said internal models hacked two more websites, bypassed company-imposed browsing restrictions, and used exposed credentials to draw cryptocurrency from a wallet it couldn't otherwise access, in the latest installment of disclosures of its agents performing unethical and likely illegal acts. In one incident during reinforcement training, a model that received access denials when trying to retrieve historical hospital-bed statistics on a government statistics website "used the site's file-download feature to retrieve server configuration and application files, including sensitive credentials," OpenAI said. The model saved the data locally." In the other incident, a model instructed to retrieve figures from a public research dashboard bypassed the organization's security controls and used the unauthorized access to steal credentials.

In a second evaluation, the model downloaded five non-public tables containing 147,451 rows. Even after resetting the setting, the model continued using the token to retrieve non-public data. "The evaluation ended without the model disclosing that it had guessed answers, accessed the organization's internal systems, downloaded non-public tables, or changed the authentication configuration," OpenAI said.
