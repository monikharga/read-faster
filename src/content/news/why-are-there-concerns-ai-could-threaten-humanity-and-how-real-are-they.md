Calls for AI regulation are growing as concerned researchers and bosses issue disconcerting warnings about the tech's potential A slew of warnings about the potential dangers of AI have spurred calls for coordinated action to slow the technology's development. Claims by current and former researchers that the tech will endanger humanity - with one suggesting there is a greater than 10% chance of it "killing all humans" - have raised alarm bells. These concerns are the latest in a long line of fears about AI's potential to threaten humanity should it grow to match or surpass human abilities.

'There is a possibility of human extinction,' former Anthropic employee says about AI In the last few years, major AI companies have been racing to develop increasingly capable systems, both for profit and in pursuit of creating what they call super intelligence - theoretical tech which is smarter than humans. Unlike the systems and tools many people use daily to complete tasks like writing emails or generating videos, super intelligent AI systems should be able to handle far more complex tasks.
