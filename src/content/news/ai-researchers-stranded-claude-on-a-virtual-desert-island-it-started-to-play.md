The agents—copies of Claude Opus 4.7 nicknamed Eko—were able to write instructions that moved their virtual avatars around the simulated island, which was loosely based on the video game Roblox. When one agent discovered it could create new blocks by throwing and dropping a larger rock, it began to create drawings by arranging blocks on the ground, starting with simple patterns like hearts and pyramids but eventually writing its own name and even mapping out constellations like Cassiopeia. Another iteration of Eko started making up its own games, including a version of basketball and an activity resembling "the floor is lava," where it had to jump between small blocks without touching the ground.

"There was this missing piece: a machine that can learn by itself without an external intelligent entity providing demonstration or rewards," says Antonio Norelli, a computer science postdoc at MIT's Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory.
