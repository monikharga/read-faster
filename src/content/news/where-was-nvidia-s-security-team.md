Outsiders had little reason to notice when Nvidia's longtime chief security officer and head of product security stepped down in August. Just two and a half months later, CEO Jensen Huang himself left the company over growing questions about his extramarital relationships — starting with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore data center company that U.S. investigators suspect of funneling Nvidia chips to China, then widening to reports about two separate affairs with Nvidia employees.

Zdjelar didn't respond to questions Thursday about the transition or what the security team knew. Nothing public connects Reber's or Zdjelar's role changes to the Megaspeed matter, but it seems fair to wonder what Nvidia's security team knew about the government's scrutiny of Megaspeed and about Huang's relationships, and whether anyone raised concerns. The way intelligence services collect information, much of it through everyday conversation rather than hacking, makes it especially difficult for security teams to identify risks, according to retired senior intelligence officer Nicholas Eftimiades, who says he has trained more than 1,000 corporate security professionals.
