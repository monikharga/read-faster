Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: New research has found that the rate of hip fractures among astronauts after spaceflights lasting more than 90 days was higher than after shorter spaceflights or among people who were not astronauts. A better understanding of bone health after spaceflight could help astronauts, patients and clinicians assess long-term health concerns, determine appropriate care and reduce the risk of injury. Drake, M.D., Ph.D., of the Endocrinology and Metabolic Bone Disease Service at the Hospital for Special Surgery in New York, is a co-author of the accompanying editorial "Bone Loss in Astronauts." He notes, "While data to date on bone loss associated with spaceflight are consistent, a major limitation is accuracy, as astronaut cohort sample sizes are small and spaceflights are rare." To overcome this statistical limitation, investigators surveyed a cohort of U.S.-based astronauts about all fractures reported during annual clinical exams and analyzed the data using Bayesian probabilistic modeling.
