SpaceX is an aerospace company that builds rockets and spacecraft, as well as running satellite services US regulators have given SpaceX the go-ahead to launch a million satellites into Earth's orbit to help build data centres in space - the first approval of its kind. The company, which is owned by Elon Musk, wants to use sunlight to power satellites that will process data for AI and send the results back to Earth. SpaceX believes the "orbital data centres" are the most cost and energy-efficient way to meet the growing demand for artificial intelligence (AI) computing power that is beyond "terrestrial capabilities".

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said in a statement that the project "opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America's leadership and growth". Why Canada is embracing AI data centres while the US pushes back Under the FCC licence, deployment of the satellites must begin no later than November 2033.
