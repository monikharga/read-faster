"Evidence indicates that malicious files were generated not only when users downloaded the programs directly from the official download page but also during the update process of previously installed legitimate programs," South Korean cybersecurity company AhnLab said. The unauthorized modifications are designed such that the malicious activity is challenging to detect when running the programs, while simultaneously retrieving and deploying a Python-based remote control malware (RAT) from an external server. Once installed, the RAT payload is capable of executing shell commands and Python code, transferring files, capturing screenshots, and collecting system metadata based on commands issued by the attacker.

"The malicious installer runs the legitimate Q-Dir or DesktopOK to lower the user's suspicion, while simultaneously downloading and installing a Python RAT package from an external server," AhnLab said.
