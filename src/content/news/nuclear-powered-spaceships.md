A successful mission would herald a new era of more efficient, potentially faster spaceflight—saving astronauts time and cutting their exposure to harmful cosmic radiation as they travel to Mars and beyond. Running a nuclear reactor in space may sound like lunacy, but it's not. And experts say there aren't any major technical obstacles to making nuclear space propulsion happen.

"I think it's a very doable technology," says Philip Metzger, a spaceflight engineering researcher at the Florida Space Institute."I'm happy to see them finally doing this." Still, developing space propulsion using reactors has been slow going. (You are creating an incredibly powerful nuclear reaction, after all.) After much wavering, it now appears that nuclear-powered interplanetary travel is a $2 billion bet NASA is willing to make. The approach "is very low thrust, but very efficient," says Sebastian Corbisiero, the US Department of Energy's national technical director of space reactor programs.
