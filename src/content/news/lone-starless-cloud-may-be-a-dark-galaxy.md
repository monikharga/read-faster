This could be one of the strongest candidates yet for an isolated "dark galaxy," containing nearly 500,000 times the sun's mass in neutral hydrogen. In the lambda cold dark matter (ΛCDM) model, structure forms hierarchically: small dark matter halos form first and then merge to build larger halos and galaxies. The model also predicts that some low-mass halos never form stars at all.

Such starless, gas-bearing halos are predicted to exist and are called reionization-limited H I clouds (RELHICs) or "dark galaxies." Since they have few to no stars, they can only be found through the light emitted by the neutral hydrogen gas—called the 21-cm hydrogen line. In this new study, Jin-Long Xu of the National Astronomical Observatories in China and the team study an isolated neutral hydrogen cloud, J1351+0039, found in a blind H I survey with the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical radio Telescope (FAST).
