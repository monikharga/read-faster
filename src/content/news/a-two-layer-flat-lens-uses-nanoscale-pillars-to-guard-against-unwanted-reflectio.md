Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences (SEAS) have engineered an advancement in metasurface design and processing that now includes a built-in, intentionally designed antireflective coating—a potentially critical step toward greatly expanding the incorporation of metasurfaces into practical devices. A longtime mainstay of Capasso's research, metasurfaces are ultrathin optical devices patterned with nanoscale structures designed to precisely control light. "The work we have done here is a first application for bilayer metasurfaces that we expect will lead to a wide-ranging impact in photonics." Though a metasurface may appear uniform to the naked eye, it is actually composed of nanoscale pillars whose dimensions vary across the surface.

This variation makes it difficult to apply a conventional, uniform thin-film antireflection coating, which could improve reflection overall but wouldn't be optimized for every local geometry, explained co-first author and graduate student Davide Cassara, who led the experiments. To solve this problem, the researchers created a special second titanium dioxide layer co-engineered with the first that provides point-by-point antireflection properties to the entire structure.
