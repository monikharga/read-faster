When a lift stops working in Chicago, frazzled building managers might get a visit from Jim Lembesis. He says he understands why staff and residents are so anxious to have things working again. "They wait and they wait," says Lembesis, a field technician for Parkway Elevators.

It's a never-ending cycle, you know what I mean?" The widely reported shortage of lift and escalator mechanics in the US, which is also a problem in many other countries, has got worse over time, he adds: "It's been like night and day compared to when I started." Lift and escalator technicians "amaze" with their ability to fix things Everyone needs a lift now and again. Sure, says Lembesis, you do encounter some dangers in a job like this. Nine times out of 10, the problem is with the doors, says Lembesis.

"People hit them with carts, and dirt and debris [falls into them]," says Lembesis.
