It issued the update after BBC News told the retailer it had been contacted by cyber criminals who said this week's breach went beyond the "basic contact details" Asos previously said might have been accessed. In its email to customers, Asos confirmed data profiles were taken but said no bank details or passwords were accessed. Later that day the firm confirmed to shareholders via the London Stock Exchange that the pop up was sent by an "unauthorised third party" and "basic personal information including name and contact details may have been accessed." The company then sent an email to customers with similar wording.

The BBC held off publishing this article to allow Asos to contact its customers first. Customers saw this pop up notification on Tuesday Asos said it is still investigating the data breach and it would "contact customers directly where we believe additional information, support or action may be required".
