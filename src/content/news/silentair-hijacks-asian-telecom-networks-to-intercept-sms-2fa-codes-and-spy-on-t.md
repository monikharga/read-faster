A newly identified spying group used hacked equipment inside Asian mobile operators to intercept the calls and texts of selected people, mostly in Taiwan. Enea, a telecom and cybersecurity software company based in Stockholm, found the group's attack sources in at least six Asian countries and named it SilentAir this week. The company has told the operators where it found SilentAir sources.

SilentAir sent its attacks into the IPX from the hacked equipment. For interception, SilentAir sent the target's home network an UpdateLocation message, or its Diameter equivalent. In rare cases, SilentAir equipment in several countries worked together against such targets, which the company said points to a single controller.

Outside Taiwan, targets were mostly in other Southeast and East Asian networks, and at times in East Africa, Central Asia, the Middle East, and Europe.
