In a new study, astronomers combined measurements of galaxies from 37 studies to create a big catalog and analyzed it to check for lopsidedness and its cause. Astronomers measure the lopsidedness of a galaxy using a method widely used in signal analysis. This gives an uneven brightness pattern, which astronomers separate into simple waves called Fourier modes.

After removing duplicates, they were left with measurements of 606 galaxies to investigate why some disk galaxies have more lopsided shapes than others. An A1 of 0 means a perfectly balanced galaxy, and an A1 of 0.1 means the brightness swings by roughly 10% above and below its average around the circle. If A2 is zero, it means the galaxy has no two-sided pattern at that distance from the center.

That means the galaxy does not have any bar or pair of opposite spiral arms. A bigger A1 means a more lopsided galaxy, and a bigger A2 reveals a strong bar or two-armed spiral pattern.
