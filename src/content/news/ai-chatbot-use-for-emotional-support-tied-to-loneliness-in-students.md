Kamilla Bonnesen, a postdoctoral fellow at the Brain and Behaviour Lab at the University of Ottawa, explains, "Of almost 40,000 students, those who leaned on AI for emotional support were much more likely to be struggling with clinical-level emotional issues, greater loneliness and a lower sense of mattering." Bonnesen also points out: "This doesn't mean that AI is causing these struggles. Among students who sought emotional support from AI, 57.7% met the study's criteria for serious emotional problems, compared with 29.2% of those who didn't use AI in this way. Students completed anonymous, school-based surveys that covered their AI use, emotional health, loneliness and sense of mattering.

Even after accounting for demographics, loneliness, mattering and academic use of AI, using AI for emotional support was still linked to a 26% higher prevalence of clinical-level emotional difficulties.
