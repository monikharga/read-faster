Individual neurons may retain their normal appearance, yet the electrical signals coordinating their activity can become disrupted. Looking at neuronal structure alone may therefore miss important changes in network function. This led me to wonder whether listening to neuronal networks could reveal subtle disruptions caused by complex mixtures of environmental contaminants.

Yet assessing subtle changes in neuronal network function directly in complex whole-water samples remains challenging. Because neurons communicate through electrical signals, I wondered whether recording these signals could provide a more direct way to assess their responses to environmental exposures. By analyzing 14 electrophysiological parameters related to neuronal firing, bursting and network synchronization, we constructed multidimensional profiles of neuronal network function.

We first tested individual neuroactive chemicals and a representative mixture, comparing iFNT with a conventional neurite outgrowth (NOG) assay that measures structural changes. They also demonstrate the value of examining neuronal network function alongside structural measurements.
