Against this backdrop, the study "Social capital and scaling in climate-oriented Agricultural Research for Development" examines how the role of social capital, understood as relationships of trust, closeness and support among actors, is documented in the scaling of agricultural innovations. To this end, Deissy Martínez-Barón, a researcher at the International Center for Tropical Agriculture (CIAT), currently part of the Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT, and at the University of Copenhagen; Anne Gravsholt Busck, from the University of Copenhagen; and Steven Dean Prager, from the Gates Foundation, analyzed 273 results reports covering 261 projects across 12 CGIAR research programs, reported between 2018 and 2021 under the former CGIAR Research Program on Climate Change, Agriculture and Food Security (CCAFS). The study recognizes three complementary scaling pathways: scaling out, when innovations reach more people and places; scaling up, when they influence policies and institutions; and scaling deep, when relationships, practices and social norms are transformed.
