Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Social media, music, religion-focused apps and other online spiritual content have become popular ways for adolescents to find comfort and connection—and explore ideas related to faith, religion and spirituality, according to a new report from the Center for Scholars & Storytellers at UCLA (CSS). "There is a real opportunity for media creators to step up and develop content that will spark curiosity, foster connection and open new pathways for young people to explore their faith in a safe environment." For its latest report, CSS surveyed more than 2,000 young people ages 16 to 24 and conducted focus groups with nearly 100 adolescents and young adults ages 14 to 24 to examine how they use media—including music, social media, online videos, television shows and movies—to engage with and express their religious and spiritual beliefs, belonging and identity. Online content also plays an important role in fostering exploration: 67% of religious respondents and 78% of nonreligious respondents said it has helped them discover new spiritual or religious ideas and practices.
