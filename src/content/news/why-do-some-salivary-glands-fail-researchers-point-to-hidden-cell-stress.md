Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: For cancer patients undergoing head and neck radiation therapy, people living with Sjögren's syndrome, people with diabetes or older people, reduced saliva production and the resulting dry mouth are far more than uncomfortable. In her doctoral research, now published in Biochemistry and Biophysics Reports, she investigated what happens inside salivary gland acinar cells when oxidative stress disrupts their normal cellular processes. "As cells carry out their normal metabolism or face stressors like radiation or inflammation, they naturally produce reactive oxygen species.

Normally, our cells have built-in antioxidant systems that act as a defense shield to neutralize reactive oxygen species and keep cells safe. "In patients with conditions such as Sjögren's syndrome, diabetes or after radiation therapy, we see this increased oxidative stress. One of the most important processes Golnarnik has investigated is how calcium signaling inside cells is affected by oxidative stress.
