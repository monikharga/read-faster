The Wiikwemkoong Unceded Territory in rural Ontario installed a small data center as it modernized its recordkeeping systems. For the Wiikwemkoong First Nation and other Indigenous communities, it is also about asserting authority over their own information after historically being excluded from its collection and use by colonizers. About the size of a single-car garage, the data center installed last year also stores the community's corporate documents, health data for residents and security camera footage.

It's about preventing anyone else from "having authority or setting their guidelines on what they could do with our data." The First Nation has been transferring all its information from a Microsoft data center in Toronto, about 190 miles (305 kilometers) south, said Michael McCormick, the community's chief technical officer. A backup data center on the mainland and public Wi-Fi transmitters are also planned for the community of about 4,000 people.
