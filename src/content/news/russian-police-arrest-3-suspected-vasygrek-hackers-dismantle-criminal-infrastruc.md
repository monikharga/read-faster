Russian authorities have arrested three suspected members of a threat group known as VasyGrek and dismantled its criminal infrastructure. The Ministry of Internal Affairs of the Russian Federation (MVD) said members of the group are "suspected of embezzling funds from the accounts of organizations, as well as creating, distributing, and using malicious software." In one September 2025 case highlighted by the ministry, the attackers are said to have sent a phishing email to an unnamed organization, masquerading it as business correspondence and instructing recipients to open a reconciliation report that triggered the execution of malware. According to reports from Russian cybersecurity company BI.ZONE and F6, VasyGrek is assessed to be a financially motivated threat actor that gains initial access by sending phishing emails with attachments disguised as financial documents.

Some of the key tools in its arsenal include off-the-shelf stealers and trojans, like MetaStealer, PureCrypter, PureLogs Stealer, WarZone RAT, XMRig, BurnsRAT, PureHVNC, PureRAT, Pay2Key, and PowerLoader, as well as legitimate remote access software such as Remote Utilities.
