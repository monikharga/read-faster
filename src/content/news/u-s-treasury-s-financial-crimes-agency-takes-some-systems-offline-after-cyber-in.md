Treasury Department's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) has been hit by a cybersecurity incident that resulted in "unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems," the bureau said on Tuesday. The bureau collects suspicious activity reports and other Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) filings from banks and other financial institutions and analyzes them to fight money laundering and terrorist financing. To support filers affected by the disruption, the bureau is extending some filing deadlines: covered reports originally due between November 18 and December 29 can now be submitted until December 30.

The service alert says urgent suspicious activity information can still reach law enforcement through the Financial Institutions Hotline, 1-866-556-3974, which is not affected by the disruption. Filing providers reported failures in automated batch submissions beginning November 18, and the bureau posted a notice about the disruption to SDTM late last week, according to a filing outages report by the markets news site The Deep Dive. Those answers matter because of what the bureau holds: reports of suspicious financial activity filed by financial institutions nationwide.
