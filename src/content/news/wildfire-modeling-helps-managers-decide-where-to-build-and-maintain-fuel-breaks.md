Fuel breaks are linear strips of land along which vegetation is cleared or thinned—constructed along ridgelines, roads and other strategic places—to give firefighters a better chance to safely slow or steer a fire. Often described as "speed bumps" for wildfires, fuel breaks rarely stop fire spread on their own, but they do give crews critical anchor points for building firelines, setting backfires or safely applying water and retardant. They have been used since the 1960s across many landscapes.
