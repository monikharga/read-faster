Semiconductor materials that can withstand high voltages, carry electricity efficiently and release heat effectively would make these devices more efficient and reliable. However, scientists still do not fully understand how easily electrons move through it or how effectively it releases heat, both of which are important properties for making reliable electronic devices. To investigate these properties, researchers at Hokkaido University and Kyoto University prepared thin films of alpha-gallium oxide using a technique called mist chemical vapor deposition.

By varying the amount of germanium, the team could study how changes in electron concentration affect the movement of electricity and heat through the films. The team first measured the electrical properties of the films, including electron concentration and thermopower, which describes the voltage generated per unit temperature difference across a material. By improving our knowledge of how electricity and heat move through alpha-gallium oxide, this research provides a useful foundation for designing future power electronic devices.
