Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: By targeting the biological processes that determine fungal virulence, microbiologists from Stellenbosch University are suggesting a promising new strategy for combating emerging fungal infections. In 2022, the World Health Organization (WHO) published the first list of fungal "priority pathogens"—a catalog of 19 fungi that represent the greatest threat to public health. Fungal disease is a serious yet neglected public health concern, especially in patients with compromised immune systems, including those with cancer, HIV/AIDS, organ transplants, chronic respiratory disease and post-primary tuberculosis infection.

Elizaveta Koroleva, a specialist at the Wemmershoek Diagnostic Laboratory, explore how targeting fungal polyamine metabolism could offer a promising approach to combating emerging and difficult-to-treat fungal infections. "Drawing on previous studies on pathogenic fungi, as well as our own research, we explore how polyamine metabolism can be targeted in biological contexts where fungi are most dependent on these molecules," says Koroleva. Their findings suggest that polyamine inhibition may be particularly effective when fungi produce virulence factors—the biological tools that contribute to disease.
