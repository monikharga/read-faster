The European Space Agency announced Wednesday that the Hera spacecraft is now studying Didymos and its moonlet Dimorphos from just 15 miles to 18 miles (25 kilometers to 30 kilometers) away. NASA's Dart spacecraft deliberately slammed into little Dimorphos in 2022 to see whether such a defensive move might help save Earth from a killer space rock one day. The impact shortened the moonlet's orbit around Didymos.

Next month, the small-car-sized Hera, launched in 2024, will venture even closer—just 12 miles (20 kilometers) away. Hera is carrying 25 science instruments as well as a pair of shoebox-sized Cubesats to survey Dimorphos even more closely and attempt to land on it. The Dart spacecraft—short for Double Asteroid Redirection Test—sent rubble and even boulders flying off Dimorphos, providing an extra kick to the impact's momentum.

More from Astronomy, Astrophysics, Cosmology Hera has begun inspecting Didymos and its moonlet Dimorphos from 25–30 km away following the 2022 DART impact. The collision shortened Dimorphos's orbit around Didymos, with ejected debris enhancing momentum transfer.
