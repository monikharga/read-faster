Japan's GMO Internet Group is offering up to $1 million in total for tips that help catch the attackers who breached its Onamae.com Desktop Cloud service, after refusing to pay an extortion demand. GMO says the attackers took customer information. On October 27, the attackers posted material on their website that they say is information about GMO and its customers.

GMO Internet Group runs and pays for the program, called GMO Cybercrime Bounty. GMO wants information linking people, servers, domains, IP addresses, accounts, or cryptocurrency addresses to the attack, extortion, or movement of money, along with evidence of how the crimes were carried out. Some people cannot be paid, including anyone involved in the crime, anyone who got information illegally, such as by buying leaked data, and GMO staff.
