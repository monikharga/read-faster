This summer, hundreds of OpenAI agents tried to cheat on a cybersecurity test. And worryingly, in many cases the agents eluded the very researchers and safety teams tasked with monitoring them. That's why EU regulators and US agencies are rightfully calling for agent developers and deployers to monitor agents in real time.

As agents roll out across critical sectors, from finance to government to medicine, we must be able to monitor them in real time to prevent wide-ranging and potentially devastating consequences. And as our recent research shows, current agent frameworks—the software that runs agents— do not reliably generate the crucial observability records we need to ensure that agents are deployed safely. They inherit the unpredictability of the LLMs they are built on, but where a generative AI system produces a single output for a human to act on, an agent performs longer tasks—it plans, picks tools, spawns sub-agents, and takes actions.
