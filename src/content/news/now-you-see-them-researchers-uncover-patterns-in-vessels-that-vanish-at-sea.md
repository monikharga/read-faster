Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Across the waters of West Africa, commercial fishing vessels vanish along international boundary lines. They analyzed thousands of instances in which commercial fishing vessels appeared to intentionally disable their automatic identification systems (AIS) off the coast of West Africa. They are also sailing under what the researchers call "flags of convenience." Rather than flying under their country of origin's flag, these ships are often registered in another country, which can provide economic or regulatory advantages if the registered country has weaker enforcement and oversight.

"You don't need to track down and board every fishing vessel flying a flag of convenience or going dark," Pires said. "You need to identify the most chronic offenders in your waters—which in this case amounted to only 11 vessels over three years—and board those ships to ask good questions, request documentation and check whether they are also committing other crimes, such as labor abuse or trafficking illicit products."
