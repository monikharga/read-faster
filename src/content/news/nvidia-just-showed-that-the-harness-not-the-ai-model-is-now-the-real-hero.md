Nvidia published some interesting new research on Friday suggesting it's the harness, more than the underlying model, that is far more important when asking an AI to do long-horizon tasks. A harness is the software wrapper around an AI model — the tools, memory management, and rules that turn a raw model into something that can act on its own. The TL;DR: Simply by using a custom harness tweaked to handle memory well and including a "supervisor" boss-like component, researchers got Claude Opus 5 to achieve a 100% score on the interactive reasoning benchmark ARC-AGI-3 — a set of 2D games with no instructions, where the model has to figure out how to play and win, similar to how a human would.

Nvidia's research is another indicator that, while model choice does matter, the model itself — the part that acts as the agent's "brain" — is a smaller part of an agentic system than many AI users realize, especially for long-horizon tasks.
