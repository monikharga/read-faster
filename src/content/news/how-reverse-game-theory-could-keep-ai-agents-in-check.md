A new approach known as "reverse game theory" aims to create incentives for powerful AI models that make following the rules the most rational choice. As AI models become more capable, safety researchers are scrambling to ensure they are "aligned" with human desires—to make sure, in other words, that rogue agents don't break the law, hack into unauthorized servers, or release poison gas into major cities and kill us all. Now some alignment researchers believe that reverse game theory, also known as mechanism design, might get better results.

Where game theory tries to predict how rational actors will behave when given a particular set of rules, reverse game theory attempts to design rules that will elicit the kinds of behavior we want to encourage. In AI, mechanism design starts from the premise that we may never be able to understand enough about the internal workings of frontier models to make sure they are aligned.
