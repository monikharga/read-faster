Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: Imagine doctors could understand exactly which cells caused a patient's cancer or whether pathogens contributed to the disease. Now, researchers at the Berlin Institute of Medical Systems Biology of the Max Delbrück Center (MDC-BIMSB) present a search engine that radically simplifies such tasks: "Malva." It is the first platform that can quickly sort through massive single-cell data using sequence information only, explains Daniel León-Periñán, first author of the study in Nature. "Like Google did for the internet 30 years ago, Malva allows scientists and AI tools to search across millions of cells in seconds—without downloading huge files, needing a reference genome or having deep computational expertise," adds Rajewsky, senior author of the paper.

It could also be transformative in helping researchers understand how health slides into disease or how and which cells respond to specific medical treatments." Single-cell RNA sequencing gives researchers a remarkably detailed view of what is happening inside individual cells at any given point in time.
