The UK government says it is working to identify any potential exposure in its online services, after critical cyber-security flaws were identified in the PHP programming language that underpins a significant number of the world's websites. The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), as well as its counterparts in the EU and Australia, earlier urged those who use PHP to apply the fixes. The company said it "automatically detected and restricted malicious scanning attempts targeting this vulnerability." PHP powers a vast number of platforms, including online shops, Wikipedia and WordPress – the content management system behind many websites like blogs.

Surrey Centre for Cyber Security Prof Alan Woodward said the flaw affects some 30 million websites. BBC explains Cyber security researcher Ryan Austin, who has been researching PHP security for the last 20 years, said he discovered the flaw in the programming language.
