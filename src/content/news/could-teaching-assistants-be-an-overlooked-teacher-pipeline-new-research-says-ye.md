by University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill edited by Sadie Harley, reviewed by Andrew Zinin This article has been reviewed according to Science X's editorial process and policies. Editors have highlighted the following attributes while ensuring the content's credibility: As school districts continue searching for ways to recruit and retain teachers, new research from UNC-Chapel Hill suggests one promising pipeline may already be in classrooms: teaching assistants. A new statewide study from the Education Policy Initiative at Carolina (EPIC) found that nearly 1 in 5 beginning teachers in North Carolina previously worked as teaching assistants.

Researchers also found that these former TAs perform just as well as other early-career teachers—and are significantly more likely to remain in the profession through their second and third years. Bastian et al, Pathways and Outcomes for Early-Career Teachers With Teaching Assistant Experience, Educational Policy (2026). Full profile → In North Carolina, nearly 20% of beginning teachers previously worked as teaching assistants.

These teachers perform comparably to other early-career teachers and have higher retention through years 2–3.
