Ride-hailing company inDrive, known for letting passengers and drivers negotiate fares, is working with Chinese autonomous driving firm Pony.ai and Nvidia to begin testing robotaxis in Kazakhstan in 2027. Earlier this month, Uber announced plans to test Pony.ai's robotaxis in London, where it has also started offering supervised autonomous rides with British startup Wayve, an investor-backed partner. inDrive, which has built its business around affordable rides, is looking to bring a similar focus to autonomous ride-hailing as it explores a market where Uber and other rivals are already making moves.

"We work with a range of partners in different markets, combining their expertise with ours to deliver a better customer experience." Nvidia says its Hyperion platform could lower the cost of developing robotaxis by giving companies a ready-made foundation of sensors and computing technology, rather than requiring them to build and integrate everything themselves. "Those savings are particularly relevant in emerging markets, where ride-hailing is already relatively affordable and autonomous services need to compete on cost," Ali Kani, Nvidia's vice president of automotive, told TechCrunch.
