Audio firm Taction Technology claimed in 2021 that Apple had infringed two of its patents for haptics systems, which enable vibrations for actions such as receiving a message or pressing a button. This tech is built into devices like the iPhone and Apple Watch. "Apple's Taptic Engine is fundamentally different from Taction's technology, which Taction's own testing of Apple's products confirmed during trial." Haptics allow people to interact with devices by simulating the sense of touch or feel through vibrations.

Apple's "Taptic Engine" system - enabling haptic feedback in its devices - first debuted with the Apple Watch in 2014. A San Diego judge ruled in 2023 that Apple did not infringe upon Taction's patents. In Friday's verdict, external, jurors did not find that Apple had infringed the patents in question willingly.
