Data centres use vast amounts of copper in electrical and data systems "I think we're at the end of copper," says Chris Sharp, the chief technology officer at data centre operator Digital Reality. He's not saying that we are running out of copper, instead Sharp, and many others in the data centre industry, are betting that it will be used less. Data centres use vast amounts of the metal: around 400 tonnes, external will go into a typical facility of around 100MW (data centre size is measured megawatts, a unit of electrical power).

Photonics uses light rather that electrons to transmit data Data is shunted around data centres in the form of electrons, which travel efficiently in copper wiring. Less heat means less energy is needed to cool data centre systems. Photonics are unlikely to make data centres any more popular with the public, but could make them a bit less energy hungry.
