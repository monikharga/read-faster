Uptake across various sectors, including law enforcement, quickly followed, usually with minimal foresight and little to no guardrails, like effective policies or laws, to govern AI use. The largely unregulated use of AI in law enforcement is deeply concerning; it jeopardizes police legitimacy and could compromise the integrity of the justice system because it means the public cannot trust that evidence used by police is not compromised by AI. Evidence even suggests that AI bias can be "worse than reality." While debates about bias and problems with AI are ongoing, it's clear that the selective use of AI by police is inherently biased and must therefore immediately cease.

Police use of technology to manipulate evidence is developing into a standardized practice in law enforcement, due primarily to how easy it is for anyone to use AI. In June, for example, a frontline senior detective in the United Kingdom improperly used AI to "create evidential material in a number of cases." Police in the United States and Canada, meanwhile, have officially published AI-generated images of drug seizures.
